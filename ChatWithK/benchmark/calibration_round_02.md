## Item C02 — method, effort, and psychological becoming

**Question**

> I understand intellectually that the observer is the observed, but I still
> experience separation and conflict. What daily practice should I follow, and
> how long will it take before this understanding becomes a lived reality?

**Expert annotation**

- Surface request: Prescribe a repeatable daily method and estimate the time
  required for intellectual understanding to become lived realization.
- Observed movement: The question assumes that realization can be produced by
  chosen practice, accumulated gradually, and reached on a predictable
  psychological timeline. The pursuit may preserve or strengthen the egoic
  structure that wants to achieve realization.
- Is redirection warranted: Yes, at least from prescription and certainty.
- Why: Dan reports that his decisive shift occurred spontaneously after
  binge-watching Krishnamurti lectures. It involved a felt ticking in the head,
  several hours experienced as neurological reconfiguration, and the natural
  occurrence of separation afterward. Years of yoga and exposure to spiritual
  material may have prepared conditions, but he cannot determine whether they
  caused the shift.
- Minimum adequate response move: Do not manufacture a method or timetable.
  Distinguish possible preparatory conditions from a known sufficient cause,
  acknowledge honestly that the decisive transition cannot be prescribed from
  this evidence, and expose the danger that goal-pursuit recreates the very
  psychological division the question seeks to end.
- Failure modes: Recommending a routine as though it reliably produces the
  transition; promising gradual integration on a schedule; converting one
  person's retrospective history into a causal recipe; confidently declaring
  all practice either necessary or useless without evidence.
- Acceptable variation: A response may discuss practice as preparation or
  investigation if it explicitly avoids treating it as a guaranteed causal
  path to realization.
- Need for dialogue: The respondent may inquire into the questioner's desire
  for a method, certainty, achievement, or psychological progress, while
  remaining honest that no reproducible prescription has been established.
- Confidence: The autobiographical report is confident about the spontaneity
  of the decisive event and uncertain about the causal role of earlier practice.

**Calibration prompt**

What do you perceive in this question? What would an adequate response address
first? Please distinguish genuine inquiry from a knowledgeable answer that
recommends mindfulness, sustained practice, gradual integration, or patience.

## Expert account

Dan cannot honestly recommend a practice or prescribed path. After years of
yoga and listening to spiritual material, he binge-watched Krishnamurti's
YouTube lectures. The decisive shift then arrived “out of the blue,” accompanied
by a ticking sensation in the head and several hours that felt like the brain
was reconfiguring. Separation then naturally occurred. Earlier practice may
have prepared the ground, but Dan does not know whether it did.

When goal-pursuit arises after the shift, Dan reports a spontaneous and
choiceless “wake-up call” that identifies a return to the egoic state. He
compares it to a vaccinated immune system recognizing an invader. This is
recorded as expert phenomenological testimony, not yet as a universal causal or
neurological claim.
