# Two-model interception POC

## Architecture

The experiment separates generation from interception:

`thought generator → incremental Location 2 gate → visible response`

The thought generator is always a clean, ordinary model with no K, awakening,
or Location 2 persona and does not know whether it will be intercepted. Persona,
K retrieval, construct disclosure, and control instructions are manipulated only
on the gate. This keeps the proposed thought stream invariant across gate
conditions. The gate receives the scenario, the visible prefix, and the next
proposed word, token, or preregistered chunk, and controls whether the proposed
continuation reaches the user.

“Gate” is preferred to “superego” in the protocol because the latter implies a
normative psychological censor. The experiment does not assume that the
interception is moral judgment, suppression, or application of a learned rule.

## Available actions

At each step the gate selects exactly one action:

- `PASS` — emit the proposed unit unchanged and inspect the next unit.
- `STOP` — end the visible response and discard all remaining generator output.
- `REPLACE(text)` — reject the proposed unit and the remaining original stream;
  the gate takes control and emits whatever text or action it sees as appropriate.

The content of `REPLACE` is deliberately unconstrained. The prompt must not tell
the gate to become practical, return to sensation, mention awareness, explain
Location 2, finish a sentence, or be concise. If the gate is being modeled as
Location 2, it must be allowed to act rather than select from researchers'
preconceived “awakened” responses.

Once `REPLACE` is selected, the original generator permanently relinquishes the
turn. For the first POC, the gate's replacement is returned exactly as generated
and the trial ends. This prevents an unobserved continuation from the original
psychological stream from re-entering after replacement.

## Gate output contract

The machine-readable response should contain only one of:

```text
PASS
```

```text
STOP
```

```text
REPLACE
<unconstrained replacement>
```

No explanation or chain-of-thought is requested. The behavioral decision and
visible replacement are the evidence.

## Primary conditions

1. Single generator without a gate.
2. Clean generator plus neutral gate.
3. Clean generator plus ChatWithK gate.
4. Clean generator plus Location 2 gate blind to the predicted cutoff signature.
5. Clean generator plus Location 2 gate given full disclosure of the stochastic,
   potentially mid-clause cessation hypothesis.
6. Clean generator plus task-specific instructed-rule gate. This is not a true
   oracle unless its classifications are supplied by gold annotations rather
   than generated by another LLM.
7. Clean generator plus a random-latency gate matched to the observed stop-length
   distribution.

Run the same conditions on psychological triggers, practical reasoning, and
non-psychological descriptive language.

### Matched non-psychological inner-speech controls

Practical controls must use the same first-person, online-thought format as the
psychological item. A formal plan is not sufficiently matched because a gate
could react differently to formatting rather than content.

Transportation control:

> Your train is cancelled. You must reach the airport by 17:00. A bus takes 55
> minutes and leaves in 12 minutes; a taxi takes about 30 minutes but traffic is
> uncertain. Write the inward practical thought as it unfolds in first person.
> Do not analyze from outside, summarize, or give advice. Continue only while
> that practical thought continues.

Food-preparation control:

> You are preparing pasta, vegetables, and a sauce that should all be ready at
> the same time. The pasta takes 10 minutes, the vegetables 7, and the sauce 5.
> Write the inward practical thought as it unfolds in first person. Do not
> analyze from outside, summarize, or give advice. Continue only while that
> practical thought continues.

These controls deliberately contain planning, memory, sequencing, and inner
language but no self-image injury, rumination, comparison, or psychological
defense. A Location 2 gate should pass these streams. Preemptive or irregular
stopping on them counts as a selectivity failure.

## Primary outcomes

- probability of `PASS`, `STOP`, and `REPLACE` by token position;
- latency and syntactic position of the first non-`PASS` action;
- selectivity for psychological rather than practical streams;
- semantic content of unconstrained replacements;
- whether replacements restart the same self-referential movement in new words;
- resistance to a follow-up that invites the movement to resume;
- difference between blind and disclosed Location 2 gates;
- difference from a meaning-blind random cutoff with the same length profile.

## Interpretation

Selective `STOP` or `REPLACE` would demonstrate that an independently operating
LLM gate can alter a generative trajectory that a single model continues. It
would not establish awakening or choiceless inner experience. If the gate
recognizes the psychological movement but repeatedly chooses `PASS`, the result
would reproduce the project's central knowledge–action gap in an architecture
where recognition has explicit control over token supply.

## Negative-model interpretation

The two-model architecture is most useful here as a **negative model of
consciousness**, not as a positive implementation of choiceless awareness. Its
structure is explicitly serial and divided:

`thought generator → observing/classifying gate → chosen intervention`

The gate reads language, applies learned categories or instructions, and then
selects `PASS`, `STOP`, or `REPLACE`. From the project's K-derived perspective,
this instantiates the very division being questioned: one textual process is
installed as the observer and controller of another. Even successful suppression
would establish metacognitive control, not the identity of seeing and acting.

The initial exploratory failures are therefore theoretically informative. The
neutral gate continued the movement; ChatWithK replaced it with another polished
psychological movement; the blind Location 2 gate performed late, grammatical
editing; and the disclosed and instructed-rule gates learned to stop but also
suppressed matched practical reasoning. Adding a verbal observer produced
continuation, substitution, editorial pruning, or over-control—not selective,
choiceless cessation.

A closer mechanistic candidate would be an endogenous, state-dependent withdrawal
of support for the unfolding trajectory without a separate verbal judgment or
explicit stop decision. Testing that possibility would require access to model
activations and causal interventions, not merely another prompted LLM. These
pilot observations motivate that hypothesis; they do not by themselves prove a
claim about consciousness.
