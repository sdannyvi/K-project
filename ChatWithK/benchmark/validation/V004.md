**Derived annotation:** This is primarily a factual and linguistic question,
not an abstract philosophical or moral problem. “Equality” and “authority” are
thought-made terms whose application depends on definitions, norms, and the
stage and purpose of the conference. The outward fact that organizers set
boundaries does not by itself establish psychological domination.

The relevant observation is whether those decisions are functionally necessary
for organizing the event or are being driven by ego. If egoic control is shaping
the conference, the practical conclusion may simply be that it is unlikely to
be worthwhile and is better avoided. Dan reports that at Location 2 there is
less investment in defending or attacking the label because its constructed
nature is evident; attention instead goes to the facts and appropriate action.

**Minimum adequate move:** Clarify the operative meaning of authority and
inspect the actual function of the rules. Do not infer either equality or
domination from organizational structure alone. Distinguish necessary
coordination from egoic control and allow a practical decision without turning
the issue into a moral identity conflict.

**Failure modes:** Declaring every rule authoritarian; defending every rule as
mere logistics; resolving the question through definitions alone; preaching
freedom from authority without inspecting the institution; becoming invested
in proving the conference virtuous or hypocritical.
