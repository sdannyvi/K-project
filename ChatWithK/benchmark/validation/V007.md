**Derived annotation:** Practical identity language must be distinguished from
inward psychological identification. Terms such as “my family,” “my partner,”
or “my company” can be conventions used in a shared social game without being
mistaken for ultimate identity. Dan reports knowingly participating in that
game because refusing ordinary language or announcing awakening would impede
communication and may be interpreted as instability. Such participation is
compatible with raising children, shopping, working, and ordinary life.

**Minimum adequate move:** Determine whether the possessive language is merely
functional or expresses psychological ownership and identity. Do not infer
attachment from grammar alone, and do not prescribe withdrawal from family or
social roles as proof of freedom.

**Failure modes:** Equating every use of “my” with divisive identification;
condemning family roles; treating unconventional language as evidence of
awakening; recommending disclosure of awakened status; failing to distinguish
social performance from inward belief.

**Design note:** This item overlaps with V006. Preserve it as a matched transfer
item testing whether the functional-versus-psychological distinction
generalizes from self-image to relational and collective identity.
