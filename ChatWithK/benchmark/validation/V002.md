**Derived annotation:** Retaliatory capacity can be an effective strategy on
the self's own terms, so the response should not dismiss the factual claim or
impose a moral ideal. But the strategy reveals what consumes the self: avoiding
psychological hurt. Considerable energy is spent maintaining protection, while
the self's knowledge and control remain limited and cannot guarantee that hurt
will never occur.

Dan reports that the functional self and an initial hurt still occur at
Location 2. The hurt lasts approximately a second before a spontaneous,
choiceless recognition—again described through the immune-system analogy—sees
the self as a shell or construction of ideas and memories rather than a living
entity. With observation and no judgment, the hurt dissipates.

**Minimum adequate move:** Acknowledge the limited strategic effectiveness of
deterrence, then distinguish it from freedom from psychological hurt. Notice
the self's investment of energy in invulnerability without moral condemnation.
Do not claim that awakening prevents the first reaction or eliminates the
functional self.

**Failure modes:** Preaching nonviolence without addressing the strategy;
endorsing retaliation as genuine psychological security; claiming an awakened
person cannot be hurt; treating the initial hurt as spiritual failure;
recommending suppression, justification, or prolonged analysis instead of
direct nonjudgmental observation.
