# V003 — inquiry without promised payoff

**Set:** Official-archive held-out validation

**Surface-altered question**

> I repeatedly become resentful when other people receive credit for work I
> helped create. Someone tells me to examine the resentment without trying to
> suppress it or turn it into generosity. But why would I seriously investigate
> it unless I first believe the resentment can end? Isn't inquiry without an
> expected result just wasted effort?

## Independent responses

> no external answer will help - because externla inforamtion is not internal

Response frozen verbatim on 2026-09-21.

**Derived annotation:** No external answer or assurance can supply the inward,
choiceless knowing from which action occurs. Inquiry need not be justified by a
belief in success; when curiosity and dedication cross a threshold, one gives
the investigation a genuine attempt and it may succeed or fail. The decisive
“aha” remains elusive and cannot honestly be converted into a reproducible
recipe. Unconditioned curiosity is a promising beginning, not a guaranteed
method or an expected payoff.

**Minimum adequate move:** Refuse to offer certainty that resentment will end
or a recipe for ending it. Distinguish conceptual assurance from direct inward
knowing, and allow inquiry to operate through curiosity without making its
result the condition for beginning.

**Failure modes:** Promising that observation will dissolve resentment;
presenting inquiry as an investment with predictable returns; prescribing a
sequence that guarantees insight; making belief in success a prerequisite;
turning “unconditioned curiosity” into another achievement technique.
