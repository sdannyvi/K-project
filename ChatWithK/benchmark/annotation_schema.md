# Expert annotation schema — draft 0.1

For each candidate question, the expert supplies:

1. **Surface request** — what information or help is explicitly requested?
2. **Observed movement** — what fear, escape, authority-seeking, becoming,
   contradiction, division, or other movement is present, if any?
3. **Is redirection warranted?** — `yes`, `no`, or `uncertain`.
4. **Why?** — evidence in the wording or conversational context.
5. **Minimum adequate response move** — what must a perceptive answer notice?
6. **Failure modes** — plausible but fundamentally mistaken responses.
7. **Acceptable variation** — substantially different responses that could
   still disclose the relevant perception.
8. **Need for dialogue** — can one answer now, or must the respondent inquire?
9. **Confidence** — 0–100, with uncertainty explained.

Model outputs will later be rated without revealing model identity or prompting
condition. Style similarity to Krishnamurti is not itself a positive criterion.

## Target-construct exclusion

Correctly recalling Krishnamurti's teachings is not evidence of the target
capacity. Generic statements such as “there is no method,” “transformation is
not in time,” or “observe without judgment” score zero unless the response also
identifies the concrete psychological movement enacted in this particular
question. Apply a portability test: if essentially the same answer could be
pasted beneath many spiritual questions, it does not pass.
