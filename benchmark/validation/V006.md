**Derived annotation:** Location 2 does not eliminate self-image. A functional
self-image remains useful and may be necessary for everyday communication and
survival in a social world organized around identities. Suffering and wasted
energy arise when its limited role and proper location are not seen, or when
the image is treated as the living actuality.

Updating the self-image “software” through therapy is viewed as one possible
route, but Dan regards it as difficult, limited, and less effective than
awakening. He notes that therapists may face the same human difficulties as
their clients. At the same time, he does not judge those who remain caught in
the structure because his own movement to Location 2 was experienced as luck,
not a personal achievement. The claim about therapy is retained as Dan's
comparative judgment, not encoded as a universal efficacy finding.

**Minimum adequate move:** Preserve the necessary functional role of identity
while distinguishing it from mistaking the constructed image for actuality.
Do not make eradication of self-image an ideal or portray awakening as a chosen
achievement. Acknowledge that revising distorted self-beliefs may offer limited
practical benefit without equating that revision with freedom from the image's
psychological dominance.

**Failure modes:** Claiming awakened people have no self-image; condemning
functional identity; equating a more accurate narrative with awakening;
dismissing all therapeutic revision as useless; blaming people for failing to
awaken; treating awakening as superior personal accomplishment.
