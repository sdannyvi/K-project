**Derived annotation:** Jealousy is not intrinsically a moral or spiritual
problem. It may motivate genuine learning and improvement. The relevant issue
is the friction, psychological heat, suffering, and expenditure of energy it
adds. If the same practical goal can be reached through a less energy-demanding
route, Dan reports that Location 2 will probably move naturally toward that
lower-friction route and a more pleasant experience.

This distinguishes practical goals, such as improving communication, from the
egoic project of using a method to manufacture awakening. The former can be
served by thought and skill acquisition; jealousy is simply not necessarily the
most efficient fuel.

**Minimum adequate move:** Do not condemn jealousy through an ideal and do not
deny its possible motivational effect. Examine its full energetic and
experiential cost, and whether learning can occur with less friction and
suffering.

**Failure modes:** Declaring jealousy always wrong; claiming it has no practical
benefit; celebrating it solely because performance improves; replacing it with
a moral command to admire or love the colleague; confusing improvement of a
practical skill with psychological pursuit of awakening.
